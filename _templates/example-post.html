Before 2017, to know that the pronoun “it” refers to “cat” and not to “street”, a recurrent network had to carry that information in a hidden state across the whole sentence, step by step. Self-attention solves the same problem another way: it lets every token query all the others directly and weigh, with learned weights, which of them matter. It is an associative-retrieval mechanism disguised as matrix multiplication — and it underpins practically every language model in production today.[1]

1. The problem: context without recurrence

Recurrent networks (RNN, LSTM, GRU) process the sequence in order: the token at position t only sees the past through a fixed-size state vector. That brings two costs which became prohibitive at scale:

  • Long gradient path. Information between two positions d apart crosses d multiplication steps; the signal vanishes or explodes before it arrives.
  • Serialization. Because the state h(t) depends on h(t-1), there is no way to parallelize over the sequence length — precisely the dimension the GPU would like to parallelize.

Attention was introduced as a patch for the first problem, still within recurrent translation architectures: instead of compressing the source sentence into a single vector, the decoder learned to look selectively at the encoder states.[2] The contribution of Attention Is All You Need was to go all the way: discard recurrence entirely and let attention be the only mechanism that mixes positions.[1]

Layer Cost per layer Sequential operations Maximum path between two positions
RecurrentO(n · d²) O(n)O(n)
Convolutional (kernel k)O(k · n · d²) O(1)O(logk n)
Self-attentionO(n² · d) O(1)O(1)

Table 1 — Comparison adapted from Vaswani et al. (2017), section 4. Note the central trade-off: attention buys a constant path and full parallelism at the price of a cost that is quadratic in sequence length — the subject of section 7.[1]

2. Query, Key and Value: the dictionary intuition

The most useful metaphor is that of a lookup in an associative dictionary. When you query an ordinary dictionary you supply an exact key and get back a value. In self-attention the lookup is soft: you supply a query, it is compared against all the available keys, and what comes back is an average of the values weighted by the similarity of each comparison.

Each input token — already converted into an embedding vector xi ∈ ℝd — is projected into three distinct roles by three learned weight matrices:

Q = X WQ
Query — “what kind of information am I, this token, looking for?”
K = X WK
Key — “what kind of information do I offer to whoever looks for me?”
V = X WV
Value — “what content do I actually deliver if I am selected?”

The separation between key and value is the detail that usually goes unnoticed, and it is essential: the criterion by which a token is found need not be the same content it delivers. The token “cat” can be found by a query looking for “singular animate noun” and still deliver a value carrying feline semantics. Were key and value the same thing, the model would have to resolve both roles in the same subspace.[3]

3. Scaled Dot-Product Attention

With Q, K and V defined, the whole mechanism fits on one line. It is the central equation of the original paper:[1]

(1) Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q KT√dk) V

It is worth taking it apart in four steps, because each answers a distinct design question:

  1. QKT — measure affinity. The dot product between the query at position i and the key at position j is large when the two vectors point in the same direction. The result is an n×n of raw scores matrix, one per pair of positions.
  2. ÷ √dk — stabilize the scale. If the components of q and k are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, the dot product of dimension dk has variance dk. With dk = 64, scores around ±8 are common; the softmax saturates, becomes almost an argmax, and the gradient disappears. Dividing by √dk restores variance to 1 and keeps the softmax in its useful region.[1,4]
  3. row-wise softmax — normalize. Each row becomes a probability distribution: positive values summing to 1. Row i says “of all the context I queried, this is the fraction of attention I devote to each position”.
  4. × V — retrieve the content. The output at position i is the average of the value vectors weighted by that distribution. No new parameter enters here — it is just a convex combination.
▼Why √dk, and not dk?
Because what needs normalizing is the standard deviation, not the variance. With q e k vectors of dk i.i.d. components of variance 1, Var(q·k) = dk and therefore standard deviation √dk. Dividing the score by that deviation restores unit variance at the input of the softmax. Dividing by dk would shrink the scores too much, flattening the distribution toward the uniform mean — the opposite error, and equally bad for learning.[4]
4. Masks: causal and padding

Equation (1) lets every position see all the others. That is what you want in an encoder (classification, sentence embeddings), but it is fatal in an autoregressive decoder: during training the model would see the token it is meant to predict. The fix is to add a mask to the scores before the softmax, with −∞ at the forbidden positions — in practice a very negative number such as -1e9, which the softmax turns into a weight of zero.

  • Causal mask (upper triangular): position i attends only to positions j ≤ i. This is what makes training in parallel over the whole sequence equivalent to generating token by token.
  • Padding mask: in a batch with sentences of different lengths, it zeroes out the filler positions so they do not contaminate the average.

The figure below shows the resulting matrix A for a short sentence, with the causal mask applied. Look at the highlighted row: when processing “it”, the model concentrates 41% of the weight on “cat” — coreference resolution emerges as a by-product of training, with no explicit grammatical rule.

Figure 2 — Attention weights from a single head for the sentence “The cat did not cross the street because it was tired” (truncated at “it”). Each row sums to 1.00. The pale cells above the diagonal received −∞ from the causal mask and, after the softmax, are zero. Illustrative values, consistent with patterns observed in coreference heads of real models.[5]
▶How to read the heat map
Always read row-wise. The row is the querying token (query); the column is the token being queried (key). The value of cell (i, j) is the fraction of the output of i that comes from the value of j. Columns do not sum to anything in particular — comparing vertically is the commonest mistake when reading these maps. It is also worth remembering that attention weights are not explanations: a high weight shows where information was copied from in that head, not the causal reason for the final prediction.[6]
5. Minimal implementation

All of section 3 fits in about ten lines of PyTorch. This is the core — no dropout, no multi-head, no optimization — and it is useful precisely because it is readable enough to check against equation (1):

scaled_dot_product_attention.py — Python / PyTorch 
import math
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def attention(q, k, v, mask=None):
"""q, k: (B, n, d_k)   v: (B, n, d_v)   mask: (n, n) boolean."""
d_k = q.size(-1)

# 1. similarity: dot product of each query with each key
scores = q @ k.transpose(-2, -1)        # (B, n, n)

# 2. scaling: keeps the variance of the scores at ~1
scores = scores / math.sqrt(d_k)

# 3. mask: -inf at forbidden positions, BEFORE the softmax
if mask is not None:
    scores = scores.masked_fill(mask, float("-inf"))

# 4. row-wise normalization: each query distributes 1.0 of attention
weights = F.softmax(scores, dim=-1)        # (B, n, n)

# 5. weighted average of the values
return weights @ v, weights            # (B, n, d_v), (B, n, n)


# Causal mask: True where attention must be blocked
def causal_mask(n, device=None):
return torch.ones(n, n, dtype=torch.bool, device=device).triu(1)
▼In production, do not write this
The code above materializes the whole n×n matrix in GPU memory. For n = 8,192 and batch 8 in fp16 that is roughly 1 GB of attention weights alone, per head and per layer. In production use torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention, which dispatches to fused FlashAttention-style kernels: the same mathematical result, without ever writing the full matrix to global memory.[7]
6. Multi-Head Attention

A single application of equation (1) produces one attention distribution per token — and a distribution, by definition, has to choose. But the relevant relation between two words is rarely single: “it” links to “cat” by coreference, to the verb by syntactic function, and to the previous token by proximity. Forcing all of that into a single weight vector is a bottleneck.

The solution is to run h mechanisms in parallel, each with its own projections and a reduced dimension dk = d/h. The outputs are concatenated and passed through one final learned projection WO. The total cost stays close to that of a single full-dimension head — the parallelism comes for free:

(2) MultiHead(Q, K, V) = Concat(head1, …, headh) WO where  headi = Attention(Q WiQ, K WiK, V WiV)
Parallel heads and output projection — Figure 3
Figure 3 — Four heads operating on the same input, each with its own projections. The mini-maps illustrate patterns that emerge spontaneously during training: attention to the previous token, coreference, local syntactic dependency and a diffuse head. GPT-3 uses 96 heads per layer; Llama 3 70B uses 64.

This specialization is not imposed: nobody assigns a role to each head. It appears as a consequence of training and is reproducible across models — interpretability work has catalogued position heads, coreference heads, delimiter-matching heads and the so-called induction heads, responsible for completing repeated patterns and associated with the emergence of in-context learning.[5,8] It is also known that a good share of the heads is redundant: in trained models a significant fraction can be pruned with little loss of performance.[5]

multi_head_attention.py — Python / PyTorch 
class MultiHeadAttention(torch.nn.Module):
def __init__(self, d_model, n_heads):
    super().__init__()
    assert d_model % n_heads == 0
    self.h = n_heads
    self.d_k = d_model // n_heads

    # one single projection for all heads; sliced afterwards
    self.w_qkv = torch.nn.Linear(d_model, 3 * d_model, bias=False)
    self.w_o   = torch.nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)

def forward(self, x, mask=None):
    B, n, d = x.shape
    q, k, v = self.w_qkv(x).chunk(3, dim=-1)

    # (B, n, d) -> (B, h, n, d_k): the head dimension becomes batch
    q, k, v = [t.view(B, n, self.h, self.d_k).transpose(1, 2)
               for t in (q, k, v)]

    # fused kernel: does not materialize the n x n matrix
    z = F.scaled_dot_product_attention(q, k, v, is_causal=mask is None)

    # concatenate the heads and project back to d_model
    z = z.transpose(1, 2).contiguous().view(B, n, d)
    return self.w_o(z)
7. The quadratic cost and how the industry attacks it

The attention matrix has n² entries. Doubling the context quadruples both the work and the activation memory — which is why “context window” became a marketing metric and an engineering battlefield at the same time. The approaches that dominate today fall into three families:

Technique Family Complexity Identical output? What changes in practice
FlashAttentionI/O O(n²)Exact SRAM tiling: never writes the n×n matrix to HBM[7]
MQA / GQAMemory O(n²)Approximate Heads share K and V: shrinks the KV cache at inference[9]
Sliding windowSparsity O(n · w)Approximate Each token sees only the w previous ones; long range comes from stacking layers
Linear attention / kernelsReformulation O(n · d²)Approximate Swaps the softmax for a factorizable kernel; still lower quality at scale
State space (SSM)Architecture O(n)A different model Abandons attention; hybrids with a few attention layers are the current standard

Table 2 — Note the fundamental distinction between the first two rows and the rest. FlashAttention is an I/O optimization: the result is numerically the same as equation (1), just computed without needless traffic between HBM and SRAM. The others change what is computed, and therefore trade quality for cost.

▼At inference, the bottleneck moves
During training the dominant cost is the O(n²) of computation. In autoregressive generation each new token performs only n comparisons — but requires re-reading the entire K and V cache of the previous layers. The bottleneck stops being FLOPs and becomes memory bandwidth. That shift is what explains why GQA, KV-cache quantization and paged attention have become mandatory in any modern inference runtime — the subject of the next article in this series.[9]
8. Conclusion

Self-attention is not a complicated mechanism: it is a soft associative lookup, expressed as two matrix multiplications with a softmax in the middle. Its strength comes from three combined properties — constant-length path between any two positions, full parallelism along the sequence, and a parameter cost independent of context length.

Its weakness is equally clear and equally structural: the quadratic cost in n. Almost all architecture research of the past decade can be read as attempts to keep the three properties while attacking the fourth. Understanding equation (1) line by line is therefore the prerequisite for critically assessing any announcement of a “one-million-token context”: the right question is never how many tokens fit, but what was approximated to make them fit.

In the next article in the series we cover positional encoding — because, as you notice on rereading equation (1), self-attention is permutation-equivariant: without explicit positional information it would see “the cat bit the dog” and “the dog bit the cat” in exactly the same way.
